Who uses EduNex

Everyone signs in to their own account; what they see depends on their role in the school and the duties the school gives them, for the dates it chooses. A job title on its own gives no rights. Nobody ever approves their own work, and nobody sees another school’s records.

EduNex SuperAdmin

The EduNex team. Runs the service for every school.

Can see and do

  • Reviews school registrations: approve, ask for information, reject
  • Manages schools, plans, invoices and payment checks
  • Loads and publishes the master curriculum (DSKP) for all schools

Can’t

  • Any school's lesson plans, pupils, timetables or relief
  • Approving work inside a school

School account

Signed in with the school's official email. Belongs to the school, not a person, and stays when the Headteacher changes.

Can see and do

  • Approves teachers, sends invitations, gives and ends duties
  • Appoints a new Headteacher
  • Billing: invoices, payment proof, receipts
  • Sets up the school in Master Setup

Can’t

  • Writing or reviewing lesson plans
  • Publishing the timetable or managing relief (unless a person with that duty does it)

Headteacher

Leads the school. Has the widest view inside their own school.

Can see and do

  • Everything in School administration, Billing and Master Setup
  • Publishes timetables; manages Smart Relief
  • Reads and reviews lesson plans in their school
  • Issues and revokes inspection passes

Can’t

  • Approving their own lesson plans (someone else is named)
  • Other schools' records

GPK Pentadbiran

Senior Assistant for Administration. Runs the timetable and relief day to day.

Can see and do

  • Prepares and publishes timetable versions; sets time slots
  • Imports timetables from photos or PDFs (AI add-on)
  • Smart Relief board, absences, relief reports
  • Their own timetable, lesson plans and relief duties

Can’t

  • School administration and Billing
  • Issuing inspection passes

Relief coordinator

A teacher given the relief coordinator duty, for set dates.

Can see and do

  • Records absences and finds cover on the Smart Relief board
  • Prepares draft timetables and imports
  • Prints relief boards and workload reports

Can’t

  • Publishing a timetable (only the Headteacher or GPK Pentadbiran)
  • Anything else once the duty's dates end

Reviewer or Panel Head

A teacher named to review the plans of a group or subject. A Panel Head also looks after the subject's yearly plan.

Can see and do

  • Review queue: approves or refers back plans in their scope
  • Reads the plans they review, and their history
  • Panel Head: edits the subject's yearly plan (RPT) and approves shared lesson templates for the subject

Can’t

  • Plans outside their group or subject
  • Approving their own plans

Teacher

Every teaching member of staff, including preschool teachers. Duties such as Class Teacher add a little more.

Can see and do

  • Their own week and their classes' timetables
  • Their own lesson plans: create, reflect, submit, templates, AI suggestions (if the school has the add-on)
  • Preschool teachers: daily preschool plans
  • Their own relief duties and slips
  • Class Teacher: keeps their class list up to date

Can’t

  • Other teachers' lesson plans
  • Why a colleague is absent
  • The relief board, School administration, Billing, or setting up the school

Inspecting officer

A visiting officer (for example JNJK or PPD). No account: just a link from the Headteacher.

Can see and do

  • Approved lesson plans covered by the pass, read only
  • Until the pass ends or the Headteacher revokes it

Can’t

  • Drafts, unapproved plans, pupils, timetables or anything else
  • Changing anything

In depth: timetable and lesson plans

The two features schools use every day. Each diagram shows the stages a record moves through; the table under it says, for every stage, who can see it, who can act and how, and what happens next.

The timetable, stage by stage

A timetable is kept as dated versions. Teachers always see the published version in force on each day; a new version is prepared as a draft beside it and takes over from its start date, so nothing changes mid-week by accident.

StageWho can see itWho can act, and howWhat happens next
DraftTimetable editors, on the Timetable versions page. Teachers' weeks don't change.Editors add, move and remove lessons; may discard a draft that was never published.An editor sends it for publishing.
Waiting to publishTimetable editors; the Headteacher and GPK Pentadbiran get a notification.Headteacher or GPK Pentadbiran publish it. Any editor may still change it (it returns to Draft).Published, or back to Draft.
Published (start date ahead)Editors see the dates; everyone sees it from the start date.Nobody can change a published version: changes are a new version.In use from the start date.
In useEveryone in the school: by teacher, by class, on phones; printable as PDF or Word.Teachers plan lessons from it; relief managers cover absences against it.Ends when a newer version starts.
EndedEditors, as history; past weeks show it.Nobody: it is a record.—

Rules that keep it right

  • A version can't start in the past, and can't be published with clashes (a teacher or class in two places at once). Classes taught together are one lesson, not a clash.
  • Publishing never edits lesson plans. Approved or locked plans keep the lesson they were made for; drafts that no longer match are listed for their teacher.
  • A photo or PDF import is checked cell by cell first (unclear cells are marked); only then does it become a draft version, and it is published like any other.

A lesson plan (eRPH), stage by stage

Each plan belongs to one dated lesson on the timetable. It is the teacher's own until the reflection; from then on it is a locked record that a reviewer approves or refers back.

StageWho can see itWho can act, and howWhat happens next
DraftThe teacher; read-only for the Headteacher and reviewers who cover that teacher, class or subject.Teacher edits all six sections, applies a template or AI suggestions, deletes it.Submitted (optional) or straight to the reflection.
SubmittedAs for Draft.Teacher can still edit it.Reflection after the lesson.
Reflection done: in reviewTeacher (read-only now); the assigned reviewer; other reviewers in scope and the Headteacher (read-only).The assigned reviewer approves or refers back, one at a time or in a batch.Approved or Referred back.
Referred backTeacher (with the comment), the reviewer, the Headteacher.Teacher starts a correction: the reviewer's decision is the permission.A correction, reviewed again.
ApprovedTeacher, reviewers in scope, the Headteacher, and inspecting officers with a pass.Nobody edits it. The teacher can ask for a correction.Stays approved unless corrected.

Rules that keep it right

  • Who reviews: the most specific reviewer the school named for that teacher's group, class or subject (the subject's Panel Head first), sharing the work by who has the fewest waiting. If nobody is named, any reviewer covering that teacher can pick it up. The Headteacher's own plans wait as “Awaiting assignment” until a reviewer is named: never approved automatically.
  • Nobody approves their own plan. Batch approval checks every plan on its own, so a plan changed in the meantime is skipped, never approved by mistake.
  • Postponed or not conducted: the reflection records why (no achievement count), the plan is still reviewed, and it can be carried forward to the next lesson with that class: planning only, never attendance or results.
  • Reminders: a plan due 1 school day before the lesson, a reflection within 2 school days after, a review within 3 school days; later than that, a named person is told. Each school can change these.

Changing a locked plan: corrections

A locked plan is never overwritten. A correction is a new version beside the original, and it is reviewed again.

StageWho can see itWho can act, and howWhat happens next
Correction requested (approved plans)Teacher, reviewers in scope, the Headteacher.A reviewer who isn't the author authorises it (choosing what may change) or declines it with a note.Correction draft, or stays approved.
Correction draftAs for a draft.Teacher edits the authorised parts.Submitted for review.
Correction in reviewAs for any plan in review.The reviewer approves or refers back.Approved, or another correction.

Rules that keep it right

  • Authorising a correction is permission to change, not approval of the change: the corrected plan is reviewed like any other.
  • Classes taught together are corrected and reviewed as one plan.
